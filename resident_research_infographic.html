Background
Why This Matters

Research participation is a core mission of internal medicine residency and is linked to fellowship placement and academic careers. Despite widespread resident interest, many struggle to translate engagement into sustained scholarly productivity.

Identifying modifiable structural barriers within the training environment is essential to strengthening research infrastructure and supporting resident success.
Aim
Study Objectives
Characterize research engagement
Identify structural barriers
Assess mentorship gaps
Measure demand for support
Methods
Study Design

Anonymous electronic survey distributed to residents and program leadership at UM/JMH Internal Medicine residency. Survey assessed:

Training level Career plans Research involvement Scholarly output (prior 6 months) Perceived barriers Support preferences Interest in resident initiative
Responses summarized using descriptive statistics.

Conclusions
Key Takeaways

Resident interest in research is strong and aligned with fellowship and academic career aspirations. However, scholarly output is constrained by identifiable structural and mentorship gaps.

Residents need: Accessible mentorship, methodological support, and structured project pathways.
Proposed solution: A formalized, resident-led research collaborative as a scalable strategy to convert engagement into sustained scholarly productivity.
